I am an economics researcher based in Chicago, focused on the empirical methods economists use to understand poverty and economic disparity across different contexts.

I began my academic path in political science and pre-law — earning an Associate of Arts in Political Science from Harry S. Truman College and completing a Pre-Law Scholars program at Chicago-Kent College of Law, where I studied core legal subjects and trial advocacy. Over time, her focus shifted from the mechanics of policy to the evidence behind it, leading her to complete a B.S. in Economics and Policy at the Illinois Institute of Technology's Stuart School of Business, with training in econometrics, causal inference, and program evaluation.

My research reflects that shift. As an NSF-funded Research Assistant at Illinois Tech, I have helped a peer study the relationship between FEMA funding and socioeconomic outcomes in Cook County, building regression models with instrumental variables. She has also conducted independent research on program evaluation and on the intersection of physical space design and social interaction, presented at Illinois Tech's Undergraduate Research Conference. I also have additional experience in corporate finance and valuation through fellowships with Wall Street Bound and the Greenwood Project.

My interest in global poverty and development economics took shape early on, while working as a Political Affairs and Lobbying Intern with The Borgen Project, engaging directly with congressional offices on poverty-reduction legislation.

I am currently developing my technical toolkit in Python to support more advanced applied economic research, intending to pursue a PhD in Economics.
